# Judge's Verdict — v3 (From-Scratch Architectures)

**Winner:** `agent-monokernel`


**Hybrid recommendation:** Build the monokernel's calendar on the microkernel's skeleton — specifically: take NAWA's demo-first ladder wholesale, but pour one privilege boundary at month ~4 instead of month ~15, and let the daemon zoo stay in-kernel for years. Staged: PHASE 0 (mo 0-2): NAWA M0 exactly — UEFI-app kernel, Arabic banner, serial, QEMU CI, HN post. PHASE 1 (mo 2-5): NAWA M1 plus one transplant: a minimal kernel/user split now — ring 3 with ONE shared flat user address space ('the yard') for all untrusted code, entered only via syscall into the POD-only AmanGate; capability tables and the SIJIL write path live kernel-side. First-party agents may remain in-kernel async tasks for velocity; the rule is simply 'third-party = WASM = the yard.' This costs +4-8 weeks (one trap path, one extra mapping, no per-agent page tables, no ELF zoo — the WASM runtime is the yard's only resident), roughly 10% of the microkernel's desert, and it converts the two claims the whole product rests on — 'the journal cannot be falsified' and 'capabilities cannot be forged' — from type-system promises into hardware facts before the flagship demo is filmed. PHASE 2 (mo 5-9): NAWA M2, THE demo — virtio-blk, FS with CoW staging undo, persistent SIJIL, vsock model relay (publicly flagged as host tooling), approval over console — with the WASM tool runtime in the yard so an interpreter escape lands in a capability-confined user space, not ring 0. Steal the micro's approval-surface design (exclusive framebuffer-region/input capability) the moment there is a compositor, because it is trivial at birth and unretrofittable later. PHASE 3 (mo 9-15): own virtio-net + TCP/IP BEFORE font perfection (the netstack retires the Linux-relay crutch; the fonts don't), then TTF + bidi + terminal-grade shaping — and ship shaped RTL output with simple input echo first, deferring bidi editing semantics explicitly. Put the vendored rustls/ring net-broker in the yard as its own task: the TLS sidecar is the perfect first native user-space component (message-driven, no shared state), and there the quarantine claim is actually true. Plan X.509 validation and a guest time source as named line items; declare the vendored-crypto exception permanent-until-funded, publicly. PHASE 4 (mo 15-24): per-agent address spaces within the yard (the real M4 — honest cost 4-6 months, and cheap now because everything untrusted was separately-loaded user code from Phase 1), aman-d as the first policy daemon extracted from the kernel, revocation v1 = lineage-tree kill (defer derivation-tree instant revoke), Firecracker image, first outside users. PHASE 5 (yr 2-3+): migrate drivers behind virtio-queue-window objects only where it pays (net first), MCS-style CPU-budget caps, aarch64 port, Naskh GSUB/GPOS as the year-3 flagship. Keep from both, verbatim: the purity charter, vsock-first staging, WASM non-JIT tools, the claims ladder ('governed, journaled, undoable' → 'isolated' only when true), monthly writeups decoupled from spectacle, NLnet at month ~9, and the micro's stance that the policy layer will be rewritten three times.


**Rationale:** The totals tie at 27, and that is a finding, not fence-sitting: these are the same OS. Both proposals share an identical spine — vsock-before-netstack, host relay flagged as a crutch, non-JIT WASM tools, the same purity charter, the same Arabic staging, virtio-only, even the same effort quotes from the same research brief. The only real decision variable is WHEN the privilege boundary arrives: month ~4-15 (micro) or month ~18-30 (mono), and each proposal is honest about the cost of its choice while hand-waving the cost of repairing it. The tiebreak goes to the monokernel because plans fail at their binding constraint, and the stated binding constraint is a solo developer's motivation over years. The microkernel's failure mode is early and terminal: a 7-to-14-month desert of cap tables and daemon protocols with no screenshots, ending in abandonment — its own risk section says so, and the graveyard of solo microkernels backs it up, while Serenity-class monoliths are the one solo shape with a proven survival record. The monokernel's failure mode is late and recoverable: a debt crunch at month 15-30, arriving when the project already has demos, an audience, and possibly funding to spend on it. A live project with architecture debt can pivot; a dead architecture cannot. But the win is conditional, and the condition is the hybrid: the monokernel as written carries one architectural lie — that a type-system boundary becomes a hardware boundary in 4-8 weeks without rewriting the agent model — and one marketing time bomb — a flagship 'capability-checked and journaled' demo whose checks and journal are, at filming time, one unsafe bug away from fiction, in ring 0. Both are repaired by moving a single minimal kernel/user split (one shared user space for all untrusted code) to month ~4 at roughly a tenth of the microkernel's desert cost, which the POD-only gate — the best idea in either document — makes nearly free. Score meanings: security_truth 9 vs 4 reflects that the micro's trust story is architecturally real today (unforgeable kernel caps, kernel-fed journal, quarantined crypto) minus deductions for untraced bulk rings, 'instant' revocation hand-waving, and an unverified TCB, while the mono's is a correctness discipline honestly labeled but still theater against hostile code for its first 18+ months. Demo_velocity 9 vs 4 reflects a filmable flagship at ~month 8-10 versus ~month 20-26 after realistic slippage. Longevity 8 vs 6 reflects that the micro ages well by construction (rewrite risk correctly quarantined in the policy layer) while the mono has a scheduled metamorphosis mislabeled as a retrofit. Both proposals share the same three under-costed swamps a judge should refuse to let slide: TLS is mostly X.509/PKI and time, which neither mentions; 'own TLS after a funded audit' means never; and Arabic's true boss fight is bidi editing semantics on a terminal grid and HarfBuzz-corpus Naskh shaping, not the joining-forms table both correctly scope at 1-2 months.

## agent-monokernel

**Fatal flaws:** (1) The load-bearing claim — ring-3 retrofit in '4-8 weeks with no ABI change' — is off by 3-5x and mislabels a metamorphosis as a transport swap. POD-only calls make the gate serializable, yes, but kernel-compiled async agents cannot be 'moved' to ring 3: they must become separately built, loaded, and scheduled user programs, which means an ELF/WASM loader, a user-side runtime, per-address-space memory, a trap path, and a second concurrency model living beside the kernel executor. That is the microkernel's 5-month M2 smuggled into M4 and relabeled as two months, arriving exactly when outside users do. The kernel and gate ABI survive; the agent programming model gets rewritten — so 'grows protection later without rewrite' is half-true at best, and the half that is false is the half the marketing rests on. (2) Until that retrofit lands (month 18+ after slippage), 'every action capability-checked and journaled' is enforced only against cooperative code: capabilities are typed indices that one unsafe bug or one WASM-interpreter escape forges, and SIJIL is falsifiable by any wild write — all of it in ring 0, including the vendored crypto. The flagship demo's tagline oversells what is enforced; the author knows it (risk #1) and concedes demo pressure will erode the one discipline holding it together. A confession is not a mitigation. (3) M3 packs 11-21 months of its own quoted line items (WASM 3-6mo, TTF 2-4mo, bidi 2-3mo, shaping 1-2mo, netstack 3-6mo) into 5 calendar months — 2-3x oversubscribed, and the likely slip victim is the netstack, i.e., the one thing that retires the Linux-host vsock relay hiding inside the 'no Linux' demo. (4) 'Durable suspension: ACB state checkpointable to SIJIL' is asserted, not designed — arbitrary Rust futures are not serializable; durable agents quietly require WASM instances or explicit state machines, which is a different programming model than the one described. (5) TLS: a 'sidecar' in a single address space is a module boundary wearing an isolation costume — ring/rustls share ring 0 with AMAN, so the quarantine language borrows meaning from the architecture this proposal rejected. And like its rival, it never mentions X.509 chain validation, name constraints, cert-store updates, or an in-guest time source — the actual hard 80% of TLS; 'own TLS 1.3 after a funded audit' is, in practice, never. (6) Arabic: terminal-grade shaping is honestly scoped, but an editable RTL prompt needs cursor/selection/line-wrap semantics on a bidi grid — the standards swamp where Arabic terminal support actually dies — and it is nowhere in the plan; Naskh-grade GSUB/GPOS against Amiri (one of the most feature-heavy Naskh fonts in existence) as a 'year-3 flex' underestimates a rustybuzz-scale, multi-year effort by an order of magnitude.

**Best ideas to steal:** UEFI-app-as-kernel, deleting the loader from the critical path entirely. The eight-verb, POD-only, no-pointers-cross-the-gate AmanGate with a CI lint from commit one — the single best idea in either document, because it is precisely what makes any later privilege migration possible at all. In-RAM SIJIL before on-disk SIJIL. The honest claims ladder: say 'governed, journaled, undoable' until isolation is real, say 'isolated' only after — this is the correct marketing discipline for the whole project regardless of kernel shape. Budget/deadline scheduling keyed off the ACB rather than timeslices. The Arabic boot banner as the identity beat of the very first screenshot. Monthly written updates decoupled from spectacle, and the year-1 NLnet application so survival is not coupled to virality.


## capability-microkernel

**Fatal flaws:** (1) Its own risk #1 is the fatal one, and the mitigation is a hope. Months 7-14 produce cap tables, sync IPC, ELF loading, and a daemon zoo — mudir, aman-d, sijil-d, dhakira-d, fs-d, shasha-d, model-d, net-d, plus the tool runtime: eight-plus protocols to design, version, and debug across address spaces, solo, with no debugger for your own kernel. Sync rendezvous chains (agent → mudir → aman-d → fs-d) are a deadlock and priority-inversion factory, and cross-address-space debugging is the hidden effort multiplier none of the estimates carry. The proposal's honest '2-2.5 years to an outside user' at the normal 2x hobby-OS slippage is 4-5 years; the stated project constraint is a solo dev who must stay motivated, so the plan fails its own spec and admits it ('if motivation collapses, it collapses here'). (2) 'Revocation is recursive and instant' hand-waves the hardest correctness corner in capability kernels: reclaiming memory, endpoints, and shared rings from tasks killed mid-IPC while live tasks still map them (seL4 needed preemptible revocation and years of expert effort; 'descendants die mid-task' is use-after-free bait). Same for seL4-MCS-inspired scheduling-context caps — budget donation across sync IPC is a research-grade tarpit; 'inspired by' will ship a simplification and inherit the debt later. (3) 'Agents cannot lie to the journal' is overstated: bulk data moves over shared-memory rings the kernel trace never sees — the kernel logs doorbells and cap transfers, not payloads — so semantic SIJIL truth rests on trusted daemons. The honest claim is 'the kernel records who could talk to whom with which authority'; still excellent, not what the pitch says. The consent-dialog guarantee similarly routes through a trusted compositor in practice. (4) M4 (months 15-20) is ~2x oversubscribed: log-structured FS (2-4mo) + WASM runtime (3-6mo) + DHAKIRA + approval surface + SMP in five months. TLS inherits the identical omissions as its rival — no X.509/PKI path validation story, no guest time source — and the 'temporary' vendored-crypto exception should be planned as permanent because the replace-trigger (funded external audit) will not fire on a solo hobby budget. (5) Honestly conceded but still true: the kernel guarantees least authority is expressible, not that it is practiced — aman-d's intent-to-grant mapping and approval fatigue can socially reconstruct ambient authority on top of a perfect capability substrate, so the product differentiator ships as 'opinionated defaults' — i.e., the unsolved part is the part users touch.

**Best ideas to steal:** The enforcement/policy split — unforgeable kernel-held caps versus a rewritable aman-d that merely decides what to mint — which keeps the genuinely-unsolved policy layer out of the TCB and explicitly expected to be rewritten three times. SIJIL fed from a kernel-held IPC trace ring, making journal integrity independent of agent honesty — this is the product, architecturally. The approval surface as exclusive holder of a reserved framebuffer/input capability, so consent cannot be spoofed by construction rather than by convention. A tool's WASM import list doubling as its permission manifest. 'Virtio queue window' as a first-class kernel object — the quiet enabler for user-space drivers with confined DMA. Scheduling contexts as delegable, attenuable CPU capabilities (even in simplified form). The Zircon-VMO simplification over seL4 untyped/retype — the correct solo-feasibility amputation. Vsock-before-netstack staging to reach frontier models in year one. Risk-classed approvals with SIJIL replay as the feedback loop for tightening grants.
