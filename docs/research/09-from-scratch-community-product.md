# Community & Product Brief — Building i3mlOS in Public for Years Before It's Dailyable

## 1. The SerenityOS / Andreas Kling playbook

Kling started SerenityOS in October 2018, post-rehab, alone, as a therapy project — and grew it to 1,000+ contributors with essentially two mechanisms: **monthly update videos** ("OS hacking" progress reels, calm and joyful, showing imperfect code) and **long-form hacking streams** where he wrote real components live. The videos were the funnel, not marketing: each one exposed a concrete, hackable surface, and watching a good programmer fumble in real time collapsed the intimidation barrier. Culture did the retention: "we build everything ourselves" acted as an ideological filter that attracted exactly the people a from-scratch project needs; issues without intent-to-patch were unwelcome; fun was the stated objective, which made the multi-year horizon emotionally survivable.

**Ladybird** proves the audience becomes the funding. The browser began as SerenityOS's LibWeb/LibJS; in June 2024 Kling left the OS to run Ladybird full-time under the Ladybird Browser Initiative — a US 501(c)(3) (public-charity tax-exempt status confirmed 2025) seeded by a **$1M pledge from GitHub co-founder Chris Wanstrath**, after an earlier **$100k Shopify sponsorship** landed off the back of the videos alone. By 2025–26 it runs on unrestricted donations from sponsors including Proton and individuals like ThePrimeagen (seven new sponsors in one strong month), pays a small full-time team, targets alpha 2026 / stable 2028, and in Feb 2026 pivoted from Swift to Rust and restricted public PRs pre-alpha. Two lessons cut both ways: audacity itself ("a browser with no Google money") was the fundable asset — and SerenityOS visibly slowed once Kling left, showing how tightly founder-audience coupling binds a project's fate.

## 2. Other community mechanics and the 2026 content landscape

**Redox** (Jeremy Soller, 2015–) survives on a duller but durable loop: monthly written updates for 10+ years, NLnet grants plus donations funding a tiny paid core, Redox Summer of Code, and a deliberately narrow hardware target. **Asahi Linux** added two innovations: marcan's deep technical writeups feeding a personal Patreon (~$10k+/mo class), and **Asahi Lina**, a VTuber persona livestreaming GPU-driver reverse-engineering — driver development as entertainment. The dark side arrived in Feb 2025: marcan resigned citing burnout, entitled users, and Rust-in-kernel friction; funding had to migrate from his personal Patreon to a project OpenCollective. **Handmade Hero** (Casey Muratori, 2014, 600+ daily episodes writing a game from scratch, every line explained) is the format precedent: audiences compound around a *standing promise* ("watch the entire thing get built, no libraries") rather than status updates. For 2026: from-scratch OS posts still reliably front-page HN when framed as artifact-plus-writeup (Philipp Oppermann's blog_os remains evergreen SEO); the coding-stream economy is far larger than in Kling's 2018; and an OS where **agents visibly do things** out-demos any desktop clone. Arabic-language systems content is a second, essentially uncontested funnel Tarek uniquely owns.

## 3. Funding a multi-year from-scratch project

- **GitHub Sponsors:** median income is ~zero; meaningful money follows a pre-existing audience (the Kling sequence: streams → thousands of small sponsors → corporate checks). Build audience first; sponsors are a lagging indicator.
- **Patreon:** Asahi shows five figures/month is achievable and that personal patronage is fragile — it dies with the founder's energy. Route money to a project entity (OpenCollective/fiscal host) early.
- **NLnet / NGI Zero (apply in year 1):** €5k–50k grants; individuals explicitly eligible, no incorporation needed; EU applicants get *priority* but non-EU proposals of exceptional quality with unique expertise are eligible. Memory-safe infrastructure and user-owned data (DHAKIRA) map directly onto their themes. This is the single most realistic grant for a solo Egyptian dev.
- **Sovereign Tech Fund/Agency (year 3+):** no geographic restriction, but funds *critical infrastructure already in use*, minimum ~€50k engagements — needs adoption evidence, not greenfield dreams.
- **Gulf sovereign-tech (year 2–4):** UAE/Saudi/Qatar sovereign-AI money (HUMAIN, Fanar ecosystem, national funds) currently buys models and clouds; nobody offers an Arabic-first sovereign *agent OS*. Approach with a working artifact and the إعمل story, never a deck.
- **Market signal:** E2B raised a $21M Series A (Insight Partners, July 2025), claims use in 88% of the Fortune 100, 15M Firecracker sandbox runs/month — the money in this decade is in *agent execution isolation*, exactly i3mlOS's home turf.

## 4. Earliest useful products for a VM-only, agents-only OS

**(a) Cloud "agent box" image** (qcow2/Firecracker on Hetzner/DO/KVM): a headless appliance whose attack surface excludes every Linux CVE. Honest buyer profile for years 1–3: self-hosters running OpenClaw-class agents who won't give them a real machine, security researchers, and homelab tinkerers — dozens to hundreds of deployments, credibility rather than revenue. **(b) Sandbox-execution wedge:** E2B-class platforms bill for isolation but all ship Linux guests; a capability-mediated (AMAN), journaled (SIJIL), non-Linux *guest image* for untrusted agent code is a differentiated component you can sell/license into that market without running a service — the first plausible revenue. **(c) Education:** xv6 has held its niche for decades because it's small enough to read; blog_os's audience proves demand. "THE teachable agent-OS" — readable source, a companion book/course, first-class docs (the Arch-wiki lesson) — is simultaneously the contributor funnel and a product.

## 5. Demo moments that travel

1. **First boot** in QEMU with a good writeup — table stakes, still HN-front-pageable.
2. **The goal-as-shell demo:** an agent is the scheduled unit; AMAN grants a capability on screen, SIJIL records it, undo rewinds it. Nobody has ever screencast this.
3. **"No Linux inside":** an agent completes one real task end-to-end (fetch → transform → write → notify over virtio-net), then the camera pans to the SLOC counter and zero vendored code.
4. **Self-hosted change:** an agent running *on* i3mlOS patches i3mlOS, rebuilds, reboots — the agent-era equivalent of Serenity compiling itself.
5. **Live time-travel:** break the system on stream, restore from the journal. End every demo with "here's the issue list for the next piece."

## 6. Honest risk data

The OSDev graveyard holds thousands of hobby kernels; survivors of the modern from-scratch wave fit on two hands (Serenity, Redox, Haiku, ReactOS, Genode, ToaruOS). Documented killers: **founder burnout** (marcan 2025 — entitled users plus upstream friction; TempleOS as the tragic outlier), **founder departure** (SerenityOS post-Kling slowdown), **scope** (kernel+GUI+browser+drivers simultaneously), and **working-but-userless** (Genode: staff daily-drive it, still niche without an app story). Survivor practices to adopt on day one: monthly public cadence held for a decade (Redox); build for your own joy first, audience second (Kling's explicit rule); a narrow blessed-target — VirtIO-only is i3mlOS's version of Redox's "few devs on real hardware by 2026"; institutions before exhaustion (nonprofit/fiscal host, co-maintainers with credentials, per Asahi's transition); a spinoff that earns mission and money (Ladybird); and treating a contributor's first PR as the real product.
